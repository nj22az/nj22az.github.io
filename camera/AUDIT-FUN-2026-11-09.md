# Pocket Camera: what is lacking for it to be really fun, and how far it is from a Nintendo product — 9 November 2026

The owner, 9 November 2026: "Audit yourself what's lacking to make this a really fun unique app to use" and "How far
are we from something that feels like a real Nintendo product?" This is the audit, written against a walk through the
app as a first-time user on a phone (headless Chromium, 390 × 844, the fake camera, screenshots in the scratchpad of
the session), the handovers of the last fortnight, and the Game Boy Camera and Game & Watch Ball as the yardsticks the
owner chose.

## The short answer

In features the app is rich (43 Looks in nine kinds, Mix, Smart Surprise, makeup, face stickers, Remove background,
the die-cut stickers of the residents, frames, papers, an album with backup, four games, an offline bundle). In feel it
is a capable **tool** with a few toys attached. A Nintendo product is a **toy** first: one idea, carried by a character,
where every tap answers with motion and sound and the first thirty seconds make you smile. By that measure the app is
about a third of the way: the ingredients that could make it unique are in (the residents, the Pocket faces, Ball with
your face, the printer, the Looks), but the house they live in is a camera app with an editor, and the character shows
up only in corners.

## What the first minute feels like

1. **The camera opens as a control panel.** 26 visible controls on the first screen: a bar with Back, settings and
   Photos, a Look chip top right, a zoom chip, Mix, a dice, a sticker button, nine kind symbols and five tiles. The
   viewfinder is a small 4:3 box in a field of cream; the Game Boy Camera's viewfinder was the whole screen. Nothing on
   this screen says who made it or where it comes from: no resident, no Minato, no voice. (`01-camera-fresh.png`.)
2. **The shutter has no ceremony.** The print feeds into the roll in the corner (nice) but the picture itself is not
   shown big, nothing is said, nothing happens to the frame. The Game Boy Camera went to the picture and asked.
3. **The Looks are powerful and anonymous.** Nine kinds are nine small symbols with no names on a phone; 43 Looks are
   tiles named "Action", "Mini", "Night". The dice is the one joyful thing here. Finding Action manga takes a kind tap and
   a scroll; nothing in the app ever suggests it.
4. **The album is a file browser.** Titles like "Action · 11/9/2026", two sample photos nobody chose, the copy "kept in
   this browser on this device", a Backup card, a Game Room card with a card-suit icon. Select and Delete are right.
5. **The Game Room is three card games and Ball.** Blackjack and Solitaire are good card games and belong to no world;
   Ball with your face is the one game that could only exist here, and it is fourth in the list.
6. **The editor is a small Photoshop.** Six tabs (Looks, Crop, Adjust, Erase, Stickers, Frame), makeup under Adjust with
   Strength and Fine-tune, Pop, Enhance, Auto. All of it works; none of it is a toy.
7. **Sound is beeps.** Synthesised ticks and a shutter; no music anywhere; the SC-90 chip and the PF-82 synth the owner
   built live on the same site and never meet the camera.
8. **Weight.** Engine ready in 0.25 s and the camera in 0.43 s here (good), but the offline bundle is 376 files and
   37.6 MB, the face pack's first run is 4–8 s, and the makeup pass is CPU at about a quarter of a second a step. The
   Game Boy Camera was a 1 MB cartridge that answered at once.

## A Nintendo scorecard

| What Nintendo does | Pocket Camera today | Score |
|---|---|---|
| One idea, polished until it is obvious | Many ideas, each polished, no one of them the idea | 3/10 |
| A character carries the product | Residents as stickers; the greeter dances; Pocket faces on errors; otherwise absent | 4/10 |
| Every tap answers with motion and sound | Ticks and a shutter; little moves except the dice, the printer, Memory's bow | 3/10 |
| The first thirty seconds delight | A camera with a strip; the print in the corner | 3/10 |
| Nothing to set up, nothing to read | Welcome, 11 settings, Look settings sheets, makeup fine-tune | 4/10 |
| Weird and specific, never generic | Pocket faces, Ball with your four faces, 43 Looks with named judges: specific. Blackjack, Solitaire, Backup, Crop: generic | 6/10 |
| Instant, 60 fps, never waits | Fast engine; heavy Looks and the face pack make phones wait | 6/10 |
| Something to collect and come back for | One secret sheet, one juggler sticker, best scores | 3/10 |
| Shareable output people recognise | Comics and stickers can be made; nothing is one tap; no animation | 4/10 |

Overall: a strong 7 as a tool, a 4 as a toy. The distance to "feels like Nintendo" is not more features; it is fewer,
arranged around a character, with sound and motion on everything, and the app's own strange ideas put first.

## What is lacking, ranked by how much fun it buys

1. **A front door with three doors.** The Game Boy Camera opened on SHOOT, VIEW, PLAY. Ours should open on the
   viewfinder full-bleed with the Look strip, and two doors: the album (VIEW) and the Game Room (PLAY), each with a
   resident at the door. Everything else (settings, backup, the editor's tools) one level down. This alone would make it
   read as a product rather than a panel.
2. **A host.** Thuan (or the cartoon Thuan) as the app's voice: a balloon on the first screen ("Take a photo. I'll be
   honest about it."), a remark on each saved photo chosen by Smart Surprise's measurement of it (dark, bright, a face,
   a cat), a cheer at ten photos, a sulk when the camera is refused. The storyteller in `johansson-town/src/feed` already
   has the residents' voices; the Pocket faces proved the owner likes the app talking back. This is the uniqueness no
   other camera has: a town that comments.
3. **Ceremony at the shutter.** The photo fills the screen for a beat with the Look's name, a stamp, a sound of its own
   (the Pocket Look gets the Game Boy shutter, the Riso a drum, the manga a brush), then slides into the roll. One
   second, every time; it is where a camera's joy lives.
4. **Sound as a product, not a beep.** A small chiptune on the SC-90 chip for the first screen and the Game Room, a
   different one-bar sting per kind of Look, the printer's whine, Ball's "Mayim Mayim" as the original had. The owner has
   a synthesiser on this very site; the camera should play it.
5. **Your face in more than one game.** Ball proved it. Space Fever's boss with your face, Run! Run! Run! with your face
   on a runner racing a resident, Memory with your four booth faces as a deck. Cut Blackjack and Solitaire from the
   front of the room (keep them behind a door, or drop them): they are the two things in the app that could be anywhere.
6. **Collecting.** Stamps for using a Look the first time, a face for each game won, a shelf that shows them (the
   Nintendo "album of achievements" done as a sticker book of the residents handing you things). Today the unlock is one
   sheet; a book of twenty small rewards, each with a line from a resident, gives a reason to come back for a month.
7. **One-tap outputs that look like nothing else.** A four-photo comic strip (the Comic Looks plus balloons, laid out
   by `comic-layout`), an animated sticker from the four booth faces, the photo-booth strip the timer could take. These
   are what gets shown to friends; today each takes several steps or does not exist.
8. **Weight and waiting.** Trim the bundle (the vendor packs and the sticker sheets are most of 37 MB; thumbnails
   alone would open the app), move the makeup pass to the GPU, warm the face pack only when a face feature is touched,
   and give the heavy Looks a light viewfinder build that is always smooth.
9. **Words.** "Kept in this browser on this device", "Auto, the photo set right by itself: on", "Frame: None; paper:
   None", "Action · 11/9/2026". A Nintendo product says less and names things ("Roll", "Shelf", "Room", a photo named by
   its Look and its day in words).

## What a Nintendo version would cut or hide

- Blackjack and Solitaire out of the front of the Game Room.
- Album backup, Appearance, Offline and Printer into a quiet Settings page; the first screen keeps Timer and Shape only.
- The editor's Crop, Adjust and Erase behind one "Fix" tab; Looks, Stickers, Frame stay as the toy's three.
- The two sample photos, replaced by one invitation from a resident.
- Fine-tune sliders everywhere they still exist (the owner's rule: sliders are a last resort).

## Three rounds that would move the score

1. **Front door and host** (two days): the three doors, the host's balloons at six moments, the shutter ceremony, the
   first screen full-bleed. This is the round that changes how the app feels.
2. **Sound and motion** (one to two days): the SC-90 chiptunes and stings, a sound per Look kind, every button with a
   small press, the roll and the shelf animating as things arrive.
3. **Your face in the room, and the sticker book** (two to three days): Run! Run! Run! with your face against a
   resident, your four faces as a Memory deck, the sticker book of rewards with a resident's line on each, Blackjack and
   Solitaire moved behind a door.

After those, the honest grade would be a 7 as a toy, and it would be unmistakably this owner's product: a camera from a
small Japanese port town where the residents talk back and your own face plays the games. What stays lacking after that
is what only a studio buys: a composer, a second illustrator, and a year of play-testing with children.
