# Pocket Camera UI audit, 3 November 2026

The owner: "AUDIT and enhance the UI, https://github.com/ctpena/asagaoui I need Dark and Light mode" (and, with it, "Add
the eyeliner and eyeshadow to the looks", done in `makeup.js`; see the end). Earlier rules that bind this audit: the app
does the job, sliders are a last resort; "simple as a Nintendo game, no need for tutorial"; one signal per concept, no
decorative dots or badges; keep it clean.

## The reference: AsagaoUI

[ctpena/asagaoui](https://github.com/ctpena/asagaoui) is "UI for beautiful and accessible digital services, based on
Japan's Digital Agency design system" (design.digital.go.jp), MIT, SCSS and TypeScript built to one `dist` stylesheet,
documented with Hugo. It is early (6 stars, 225 commits, "the main branch is for development and is not stable", no
release), its stylesheet names its colours in place (the Digital Agency blue, Noto Sans JP), and it has no dark mode of
its own. So it is not a dependency and nothing from it was copied (AGENTS.md: study, measure, never copy). What was
taken from it and from the design system behind it is the checklist a public-service UI is held to:

- one set of named colour tokens, used everywhere, with a light and a dark value each;
- text and chosen states at 4.5:1 or better against what they sit on;
- a visible focus ring on every control, 44-point targets;
- plain words, one way to say a thing, nothing to learn.

Pocket Camera already keeps the last two (every button is at least 44 points, `camera-ui.css`; the focus ring is three
points of the accent). The first two it did not, and that is where the findings are.

## Method

Every screen was photographed before and after at a phone (390 × 844, 2×) and a desktop (1280 × 900): the welcome, the
camera settings, the editor's Looks, Crop, Adjust (with Makeup open on a found face, with Face stickers open), Erase,
Stickers and Frame tabs, the saved photo, the album, the backup sheet, the Game Room, Blackjack, Solitaire and Memory,
in light and in dark (`tests/theme.browser.mjs` now measures the contrast of the chosen chips and labels in both). The
grades: **A** must change, **B** should change, **C** later or by choice. Everything graded A or B is done in this
release.

## Findings

| # | Grade | Finding | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | **Four stacked palettes, no tokens.** `camera.css`, `camera-refresh.css`, `camera-black.css` and `camera-ui.css` each declared their own `:root` (iOS black, island teal, pure black, graphite); the last two won, the teal layer was dead, and the chrome named its colours in place 50-odd times (`#0d0e10` bars, `#121315` tab bar, `#383225` chosen tab, `#141619ed` compare chip, `#fff`/`#000` chips…). A theme could not be added without touching every file. | One token set in `camera.css` `:root` (the live dark values), the light set under `html[data-theme=light]`, and every chrome colour in all four files is now a token: `--bg --bar --surface --surface-2 --well --hero --fill --fill-strong --label --label-2 --label-3 --separator --outline --accent --accent-fill --on-accent --accent-soft --chip-on --chip-on-label --red --stamp --shadow --overlay --shutter --shutter-ring`. The other three files keep only type, radii and layout. |
| 2 | A | **No light mode.** The page declared `color-scheme: dark` and a black theme colour; a phone set to light got a black camera. | **Appearance** in the camera settings: Auto (follows the device, the default), Light, Dark; remembered in the camera's own settings. A head script sets `data-theme` before the first paint so a stored choice never flashes; `app.js applyTheme` follows a device change while on Auto and sets the browser's theme colour (`#fbfaf7` light, `#0d0e10` dark). |
| 3 | A | **The yellow does not read on white.** The camera's yellow is what is chosen (tab, tile ring, switch, rail chip). On a light surface yellow text is 1.4:1. | Two tokens where there was one: `--accent-fill` stays the yellow for the one main button (Save copy, Start camera) with ink on it; `--accent` is what is chosen and reads in a deep amber (`#8a6400`) in light: 5.4:1 on white, 4.5:1 on the deepest surface, 11.7:1 as the yellow on dark. |
| 4 | A | **Chosen crop shape unreadable.** `camera-black.css` set the chosen chip's text to near-black and `camera-ui.css` set the Crop chips' background to the dark surface after it: "Original" was dark on dark (1.5:1) and looked disabled. | The chosen shape is the chosen chip (`--chip-on`) like every other chosen chip. |
| 5 | A | **iPhone install, light theme, white status bar text.** The page uses `black-translucent`, so the time and battery are white over the page. | In light the band behind the status bar stays dark (`html.standalone[data-theme=light]`), so they read; the rest of the page is light. Unverified on a physical iPhone (see below). |
| 6 | B | **A ring around every album card.** A leftover of the island layer drew a faint 1-point ring round each album card, hugging the caption. | Removed. |
| 7 | B | **Makeup wore the Adjust tab's icon.** The Makeup button and the Adjust tab both showed the sliders glyph. | A lipstick (`#i-makeup`). The Appearance row uses the half-disc already used for Contrast (one glyph, two screens apart). |
| 8 | B | **The games in light.** The Game Room, Memory, Blackjack and Solitaire are illustrated in paper and harbour ink (`game-room-refresh.css`), which `camera-black.css` painted black on every screen. | In light the paper palette shows as drawn; the black stays dark-only (`:root:not([data-theme=light])`). The album's two entries (Album backup, Game Room) follow. |
| 9 | C | **Two doors in a row.** In Adjust with Makeup or Face stickers open, "Fine-tune" and "More adjustments" stack as two chevron rows with a rule between. | Leave: each door belongs to its row, and the owner asked for the manual controls behind doors. Revisit if a third door ever appears. |
| 10 | C | **Small type.** Tile names are 12 points, the rail's chip names 10, the sticker tab names 11.5 at 390 points. The Digital Agency system asks 14 for labels. | Leave for now: they are secondary names under a picture that is the real label, and every one has a full accessible name. A larger-type pass would want the strip's tile size with it. |
| 11 | C | **Desktop Adjust is mostly empty.** At 1280 wide the right column holds four rows and air. | Leave: a panel, not a page; the photo has the room. |
| 12 | C | **`camera-refresh.css` is dead weight.** Its palette is overridden; only type weights, radii and shadows survive. | Kept, annotated, so the published bundle keeps its file list stable this release; fold the survivors into `camera-ui.css` and delete it next time the CSS is touched. |
| 13 | C | **Site theme and camera theme are separate.** The site's `nj-theme` (shared.js) is not read by the camera, which is its own installed app. | By choice: Auto follows the device, which is what a visitor set the site from in the first place. If the owner wants the camera to follow the site's toggle instead, read `nj-theme` in the head script's fallback. |

What was checked and found right: 44-point targets on every control, a focus ring on every control, labels only on the
chosen tab (the others have accessible names and titles), one yellow signal per concept with no second marker, the hints
in plain words ("Pinch to zoom. Drag to position.", "Paint over what should go.", "Set for this photo."), the Original
compare and the rail in both themes, nothing that needs a tutorial.

## Eyeliner and eyeshadow

Two more masks from the face pack's landmarks, in `makeup.js faceMasks`: the **liner** is the upper lash line (the eye
loop's first nine points, outer corner first), thin at the inner corner and fuller at the outer, carried past the corner
and lifted toward the brow as a wing when the look asks (0 none, 1 a full cat eye), never on the eye; the **shadow** is a
wash from the lash line up over the lid, highest over its middle, a little past the outer corner, feathered toward the
brow, kept off the eye, the brow and the band under the eye, and gated to skin-coloured pixels so lashes and hair are
left alone. Tones are moves from the person's own skin colour in OKLab (taupe, smoke, bronze, rose, plum, gold, peach),
and the liner draws toward black or brown. Each look names both: Natural a light brown line and a taupe wash, Fresh
peach, Clean neither, Soft glam a black line with a small wing and rose, Evening a full wing and smoke, Bold lip a wing
and taupe, Sun-kissed brown and bronze, Rose brown with a hint of wing and rose. Both follow Strength and the Eyes
fine-tune. The sheet of the eight looks on the test face and a close crop of the eyes are in the handover.

## Verification

`tests/theme.browser.mjs` (5 checks): Auto follows the device both ways with the theme colour; the setting pins Light
and Dark and is remembered; a stored choice is set before `app.js` runs; the chosen tab, the chosen crop shape, labels and
hints are at 4.5:1 or better in both themes; the games are paper in light and black in dark. `tests/makeup.test.mjs`
(6): the liner darkens the lash line toward its tone, the wing reaches past the corner, the wash is a wash and not a
paint, Clean and Eyes 0 draw neither. The whole camera regression (every suite in `.github/workflows/pocket-camera.yml`,
now with `theme`) is recorded in the handover.

**Open:** a physical iPhone, in Safari and installed, in both themes (the status-bar band in particular); a desktop
Safari and Firefox pass of the `:is()` and `:root[data-theme]` selectors (Chromium only here).
