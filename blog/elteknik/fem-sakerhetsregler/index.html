Before you open the switchboard

Five safety rules under SS-EN 50110-1

Nils Johansson · 23 September 2026 · 3 min read

The circuit-breaker is open and the indicator is dark. It is tempting to undo the cover and begin. But an unlit lamp proves nothing, and on board there is often more than one supply route into a switchboard.

The main principle: work dead

SS-EN 50110-1, and the Swedish electrical safety instructions (ESA) based on it, take working in the dead condition as the first choice. Five rules establish and maintain that condition, in this order:

  1. Disconnect completely. Isolate every supply to the work location, not just the one you know about.
  2. Secure against reconnection. Lock the switching device and attach a notice so that nobody else can switch on while you work.
  3. Verify that the installation is dead. Test at the work location, between all phases and to earth. Check the voltage detector against a known voltage source before and after the test.
  4. Earth and short-circuit where required: always in high-voltage installations, and at low voltage where there is a risk of reconnection or induced voltage.
  5. Protect against adjacent live parts by screening or barriers.

Why ships are different

Rule 1 is more demanding at sea because a switchboard may receive power from several directions:

  • Several generators that can be connected to the same busbar.
  • The emergency generator, which starts automatically and energises the emergency switchboard if the main switchboard loses power.
  • Shore power while the vessel is alongside.
  • Batteries and UPS systems supplying emergency lighting, control systems and communications.
  • An alternative supply from another switchboard through changeover devices.
  • Capacitors, such as those in a variable-frequency drive's DC link, which can retain dangerous voltage after disconnection. Wait for the manufacturer's specified discharge time, then measure.

Safe work therefore starts with the diagram: which supply routes reach this particular section, and how is each one isolated?

Roles and communication

The standard distinguishes between the person responsible for the electrical installation and the person in control of the work activity. Disconnection and reconnection must be coordinated between them, with a clear handover: who has disconnected the supply, what has been secured, and when may the installation be re-energised? On board, with watch changes and several jobs running at once, that handover is often the weakest link.

Key points

  • An unlit indicator is not a test for absence of voltage.
  • Identify every supply route, including the emergency generator, shore power, batteries and capacitors.
  • Prove the voltage detector on a known source before and after testing.

This article is an introduction. It does not replace training, the installation's own instructions or applicable regulations.
