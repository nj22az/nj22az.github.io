Three-phase power at 440 V and 60 Hz

Star, delta and the power in a pump motor

Nils Johansson · 23 September 2026 · 3 min read

Sweden's shore supply is 400 V at 50 Hz. On many ships, the main supply is 440 V at 60 Hz. The physics is the same, but the numbers differ, and applying familiar shore-based assumptions can easily produce the wrong current or speed.

Line voltage and phase voltage

In a three-phase supply, line voltage UL is the voltage between two lines, while phase voltage Uph is the voltage between a line and the neutral point. They are related by the factor √3:

UL = √3 · Uph

At 400 V, the phase voltage is 231 V. At 440 V it is 440 / √3 ≈ 254 V.

Star and delta

A three-phase load has three windings, which can be connected in two ways:

  • Star (Y): each winding is connected between a line and a common neutral point. The winding receives the phase voltage, UL / √3, and the line current equals the winding current.
  • Delta (Δ): each winding is connected between two lines. It receives the full line voltage, and the line current is √3 times the winding current.

This is the principle behind star–delta starting. A motor intended to run in delta is started in star, so that each winding receives only the line voltage divided by √3. The starting current drawn from the supply and the starting torque are then approximately one third of their values when starting directly in delta. Once the motor has gained speed, it is switched to delta.

Power in a pump motor

The active power of a balanced three-phase load is:

P = √3 · UL · I · cos φ

Here, I is the line current. The formula applies whether the load is connected in star or delta. Consider a 440 V pump motor drawing 30 A at a power factor of 0.85:

Apparent power S√3 · 440 · 30 ≈ 22.9 kVA
Active power P22.9 · 0.85 ≈ 19.4 kW
Reactive power Q√(22.9² − 19.4²) ≈ 12.0 kvar
Shaft power at 90% efficiency19.4 · 0.90 ≈ 17.5 kW

Two common errors are forgetting √3, or mixing phase voltage with line current. Keep the quantities together: line voltage with line current and √3; or winding voltage with winding current, without √3.

What 60 Hz means for speed

An induction motor's synchronous speed is:

ns = 60 · f / p

Here, p is the number of pole pairs. A four-pole motor, with two pole pairs, has a synchronous speed of 1,500 r/min at 50 Hz but 1,800 r/min at 60 Hz. Its actual speed is slightly lower because of slip. A centrifugal pump's power demand increases approximately with the cube of speed, so a 20% increase in speed produces roughly a 70% increase in power demand. Equipment moved between 50 Hz and 60 Hz must therefore always be checked against its nameplate.

Key points

  • A line voltage of 440 V gives a phase voltage of approximately 254 V.
  • P = √3 · U · I · cos φ uses line voltage and line current, regardless of the connection.
  • 60 Hz gives a synchronous speed 20% higher than 50 Hz. Check the nameplate.
