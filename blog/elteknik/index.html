Five-part series

Electrical engineering on board

Five practical problems on ships and in industry, with the electrical engineering needed to solve them.

Worked examples connect theory to a dim navigation light, a three-phase motor, safe isolation, an insulation alarm and a pump that will not start. The series is based on the Swedish textbook Elteknik och ellära för sjöfart och industri.

  1. Part 1

    The navigation light that glows dimly

    Why does a lamp glow dimly when the battery reads 24 V? Cable resistance, a corroded terminal and the right way to measure.

  2. Part 2

    Three-phase power at 440 V and 60 Hz

    Line and phase voltage, star and delta connections, and a worked example of three-phase power in a shipboard pump motor.

  3. Part 3

    Before you open the switchboard

    The five basic rules for working dead, and why a shipboard switchboard can have more supply routes than you expect.

  4. Part 4

    The insulation alarm that will not clear

    Why the first earth fault does not trip a shipboard IT supply, why the alarm returns, and how to locate the fault before a second occurs.

  5. Part 5

    The pump that will not start

    From symptom to cause in five steps: separate power and control circuits, narrow the search, measure safely and establish why the protection tripped.
