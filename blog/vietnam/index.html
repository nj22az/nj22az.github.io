Reflections

Vietnam

Nils Johansson · 10 November 2025

Where Chaos and Calm Share the Same Sidewalk

Vietnam hits you in two ways at once. First, the chaos — scooters weaving like a murmuration of starlings, horns speaking their own language, markets bursting with color and noise. Then, suddenly, the calm — a cup of cà phê sữa đá under a palm, fishermen untangling nets at sunrise, a grandmother humming folk songs while slicing morning glory into a bowl.

Street Food: The Real Capital of Vietnam

Hanoi has government offices, but the true capital is a tiny plastic stool on the sidewalk. You sit down, somebody hands you something steaming, fragrant, and mysterious. No menu. No fuss. Just trust. The rule is simple: if the broth has been simmering since dawn, it will change your life.

“In Vietnam, food isn’t served — it’s offered.”

Favorites you can’t leave without trying:

  • Phở bò Hà Nội — slow broth, clean flavors, eaten at 6 AM like a local.
  • Bánh mì Hội An — heritage French bread meets Vietnamese soul.
  • Bún chả — chargrilled pork, fish sauce, and fresh herbs that taste like summer.

The Magic of Coffee Culture

In Vietnam, coffee isn’t a drink — it’s a ritual. Egg coffee in Hanoi tastes like a tiramisu that went back to college and studied caffeine. In Saigon, iced coffee drips so slowly that time becomes optional. Nobody is rushing you out of your chair. Stay. Think. Dream.

Endless Motion, Endless Heart

People move with purpose — buying ingredients three times a day because food is not storage, it’s connection. Neighbors talk. Everyone has an uncle on a scooter who can transport anything: a fridge, a family of five, and sometimes both at once.

From Mountains to Coast

One country, multiple worlds:

  • Sapa — mist rolling off the mountains and rice terraces like scales of a living dragon.
  • Hội An — lanterns reflected in the river like warm memories.
  • Đà Nẵng — a beach city that feels like Miami, if Miami served phở at 4 AM.

Why Vietnam Stays With You

When you leave, Vietnam doesn’t leave you. It follows you home — in the smell of grilled scallions, the reflex to say “dạ” instead of “yes,” the sudden craving for fish sauce at midnight.

It’s not a place you visit. It’s a place you carry.
