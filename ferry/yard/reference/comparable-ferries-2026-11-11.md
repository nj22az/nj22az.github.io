# Real ferries of her size: proportions, lifeboats, and what the rules ask

Johansson Shipyard · reference · gathered 11 November 2026 for the owner's "try to make the ship more realistic in
proportions", "it needs life boats" and "need to build it as realistic as possible"

Gathered by a research agent from public sources and read back here as it reported. How far each figure can be trusted:

- **[Sn]** is a source in the list at the end.
- **photo est.** is a measurement taken off a broadside photograph and scaled by the ship's length: about ±1 m.
- **n/v** means not verified in any source that could be reached.
- Rule texts: SOLAS was **read** (the 2018 consolidated edition as hosted by the Icelandic Transport Authority, S15), and the
  Danish notice (S16). The UK regulation was read only through an extract (S17).

Nothing here has been built on yet.

**The pattern.** The ships of 1974 to 1989 carry lifeboats in davits. The British ships under 75 m built after 2000 carry
none: one fast rescue boat, rafts and evacuation slides.

**How the Chisum as built today compares** (the yard's own reading, 11 November 2026):

- Her beam of 13.6 m gives a length to beam of 5.03: that of the 1974 ships. Her depth of 5.0 m and draught of 3.2 m match
  the Hebridean Isles.
- **Her funnels are far too tall and stand too far aft**: their tops are 21.5 m above the keel where the top of her
  wheelhouse is 15.2 m, and they stand at her stern. Real funnels end at or under the wheelhouse top and stand over the
  machinery.
- **Her car deck is low for lorries**: 4.3 m clear, where the ships above have 4.8 to 5.1 m.
- **She has no lifeboats**, and nothing of a boat deck.
- Her bridge front, 0.21 of her length from the stem, is where a real one is.

## 3. Lifeboats

**What the rules ask** (SOLAS chapter III, read: S15):

- III/21.1.1, international voyages: lifeboats on each side for half of all on board (rafts may stand in down to 37.5 % a
  side), and rafts for a further quarter.
- III/21.1.2, **short international voyages: lifeboats for at least 30 % of all on board**, equally on each side as far as
  practicable; rafts to make up 100 %; and rafts for a further 25 %.
- III/21.1.3: every survival craft launched fully loaded within 30 minutes.
- III/21.2: of 500 gross tons and over, a rescue boat on each side; a lifeboat may be one if it meets a rescue boat's rules.
- III/21.3: each lifeboat or rescue boat marshals at most 6 rafts (9 on short international voyages).
- III/26, ro-ro passenger ships: one rescue boat at least is a fast rescue boat; rafts have boarding ramps.
- III/13, 23, 24: the embarkation position at least 2 m above the loaded waterline; a davit's head no more than 15 m above
  the lightest waterline as far as practicable.
- European domestic ships (the Danish notice of 2003, S16): survival craft for 1.25 times all on board, which may all be
  rafts; one rescue boat, a fast one on a ro-ro passenger ship.

**For 362 on board, short international:** lifeboat seats for at least 109 (one boat of 55 to 61 persons on each side),
about 340 places in rafts, a rescue boat on each side.

**How ships of 60 to 85 m carry them:** one boat a side on the boat deck abreast the funnels at mid-length (Claymore); boats
in gravity davits abreast and forward of the funnels with a walkway past them (Isle of Arran); two a side between 0.42 and
0.62 of her length, their keels about 14.8 m above the water, one deck above the passenger deck (Lord of the Isles).

## 4. What makes the profile look right

- The dark hull is carried up over the car deck's sides, and the white house begins at the passenger deck. Portholes in
  the hull, rectangular windows in the white.
- The bow rises by a step or sheer into a forecastle with a bulwark, which holds the mooring gear over the visor.
- The visor's seam, a recessed anchor pocket, the thruster's marks and the draught marks can all be seen.
- A rubbing strake, or two (at about 1.8 m and 6.4 m above the water on Lord of the Isles).
- One tier is dropped at each step going aft. The funnel's top stays at or below the wheelhouse top; the mast stands
  about 7 m over it.
- Bridge front windows lean outward at the top by 10 to 25°, with 225° of view and the ship's side seen from the wing,
  which is why wheelhouses reach the full beam (SOLAS V/22, read).
- Standard window sizes (ISO 3903, from a preview only): 355 × 500, 400 × 560, 450 × 630, 500 × 710, 560 × 800,
  900 × 630, 1000 × 710, 1100 × 800 mm.

## Not reached or not verified

Japanese coastal ferries (one partly: Ferry Zamami, 1998, 61.0 × 11.3 m, 380 passengers, S14); deck to deck height of a
vehicle deck; the width of promenades and side decks; passenger ceiling heights; stored-power and telescopic davits; the
current European directive's text.
