# Ro-ro freight ships: real ships, their arrangements and where their drawings can be seen

This reference was gathered on 12 November 2026 by a research agent from public sources. Every figure names its source
through the bracketed keys listed below. "n/v" means not verified: either no source reached gave the figure, or the only
basis is an inference that is stated as such. Nothing here is invented to fill a gap. The drawings named here belong to
their owners (shipyards, operators, investigation boards, a university): they are to be studied and measured, never copied.

## How to read this

Source keys used in tables and text. Full URLs are in section 6.

- [MR] MAIB report 18/2009, grounding and loss of the ro-ro cargo vessel Riverdance (built 1977 as Mashala).
- [MRA] The annexes to [MR], chiefly the QinetiQ stability study.
- [MM] MAIB report 5/2009, grounding of Moondance (built 1978 as Emadala, sister of Riverdance).
- [MG] MAIB report on Stena Gothica (built 1975 at Sandefjord, Tor Gothia class), Immingham, 2 April 2002.
- [SHK] Swedish Accident Investigation Board report RS 2008:03e, loss of Finnbirch (Stena Searunner class, 1978).
- [GA-R] my own reading of the general arrangement drawing printed as Figure 1 of [MR]. Labels were read from
  enlargements of a 981 x 1371 pixel scan: deck names and the particulars table are certain, room names are "as read".
- [B] Bore Ltd vessel data sheets (July 2019), one PDF per ship, each with a general arrangement on page 2.
- [LNU] A teaching ro-ro plan at scale 1:200 ("M/V Beforeteller", 2011) on Linnaeus University's course server.
- [W] Weco RoRo ship sheet for Frijsenborg (2016), as hosted on naviecapitani.it.
- [K] Knud E. Hansen product sheets (European Pioneer ex Bison; Stena Searunner; Sea Centurion; Tor Selandia).
- [F] faktaomfartyg.se ship page (Swedish ship database). Dimensions are printed as length x breadth x draught,
  tonnage as Brt/Nrt/Dwt, power in kW unless "hk" (metric horsepower) is printed.
- [P] my own reading of a photograph on faktaomfartyg, or of the photograph on a [K] sheet.
- [D] doverferryphotosforums.co.uk "past and present" ship page.
- [FS] ferry-site.dk vessel page.
- [SA] H. Ando (Kawasaki Heavy Industries), paper "Seaspeed Arabia and Seaspeed Asia, the largest ro-ro ships in
  service" (1977), a six-page scan in the US MARAD vessel history database. Text only: its figures are not in the scan.
- [MRep] Maritime Reporter articles of 1978 (Dana Maxima, 15 October; Boogabilla, December), read through a page summary.
- [N] Cruise & Ferry Info, June 2001, article on Stena Forwarder (Visentini), on the NAOS design site.
- [WE] Wärtsilä Encyclopedia of Marine and Energy Technology, entry "Ro-ro freighter Tor Magnolia".
- [IMO] IMO Assembly resolution A.581(14), 1985, securing arrangements for road vehicles on ro-ro ships.
- [E] the companion file `classic-ferries-1958-1978.md` in this folder (Estonia report and others), for comparison only.
- "(summary)" after a key means the fact was seen only in a search engine's summary of that page, not on the page itself.

Units: lengths in metres, power in kW unless stated. "Lane metres" are as each source prints them: sources do not say
which lane width or which decks they count, so figures from different sources are not comparable.

### 1b. What drawing 1 (Riverdance) says, as read [GA-R]

General particulars printed on the drawing: length over all 116.30 m; length between perpendiculars 103.40 m; breadth
moulded 17.40 m; depth to 1st deck 12.20 m; depth to 2nd deck 5.70 m; draught (freeboard) 5.379 m. [MM] gives the same
length, breadth 17.40 m and depth 12.2 m. [F] prints 116.31 x 18.22 x 5.38 m: 18.22 m is then the extreme breadth (my
inference). The deck-to-deck height between the main and the upper deck is therefore 6.50 m moulded.

The profile carries the word "Pandoro" on the hull. Riverdance was not on Pandoro charter in 1995 according to her [F]
history, so the sheet was probably drawn by Cammell Laird for a sister ship and reused by MAIB (my inference). Sisters per
[F]: Emadala, Salahala, Gabriele Wehr, Thomas Wehr.

- **Profile.** Accommodation forward in four superstructure decks; a large pyramid-shaped funnel casing about one third
  of the length from the stern; mushroom ventilators on the after upper deck; a bulbous bow; the engine room aft under the
  2nd deck; a lower hold under the 2nd deck forward of the engine room; an internal fixed ramp from the 2nd deck up to
  the 1st deck drawn in dashed lines.
- **4th superstructure deck.** Wheelhouse top only.
- **3rd superstructure deck.** "Wheelhouse" on the centre, a position labelled "Controls" at each bridge wing.
- **2nd superstructure deck.** Starboard side from forward: Captain, Chief Officer, 2nd Officer, Officer (spare). Port
  side from forward: Chief Engineer, 2nd Engineer, Electrician and 3rd Engineer. On the centre: Ship's Office, "Air
  Conditioning" and a small room read as dispensary. A separate after block holds Cabins No. 3, 4, 5 and 6 and a
  stairwell. A "Motor Life Boat" is drawn outboard of that block on each side.
- **1st superstructure deck.** Along the front: Cook, five rooms labelled Seaman, Bosun. Starboard side: Galley, Crew's
  Mess, Crew Recreation Room, Pantry, Officers' Mess. Centre: a block of provision rooms (refrigerated room and stores).
  Port side: more crew cabins and, aft, two rooms read as emergency generator room and a store or CO2 room (uncertain).
  The after block holds Cabins No. 1 and 2, "Pax Lounge Area", "Pax Dining Area" and the stairwell.
- **1st deck** (upper, weather deck). Clear trailer deck from the stern to the house; mooring winches at the stern; small
  casings at the sides; a long ramp opening on the centre. The funnel stands on the starboard side in photographs [P].
- **2nd deck** (main deck). Stern door; a rectangular opening on the centre aft drawn with a cross (a hatch or a lift, its
  label not legible); side doors port and starboard with a label read as pilot door and bunker station (uncertain);
  forward, a ramp down to the lower hold, then stores, chain lockers and the fore peak water ballast tank.
- **Lower tween deck.** A short deck forward only, with stores each side.
- **Platform deck, Engine Room.** "Engine Control Room" and "Engine Workshop" side by side at the forward end of the
  engine room, stores outboard, tanks along both sides.
- **Tank top.** From aft: No. 9 water ballast (aft peak), "Steering Gear", No. 8 and No. 7 water ballast port and
  starboard, the engine room with two main engines and gearboxes on the centre, then heavy oil and water ballast tanks in
  the double bottom and wings under the lower hold, and the bow thruster forward. [MR] names the heeling tanks as No. 13
  port and starboard and the permanent ballast as No. 3 port and starboard.

### 1c. What drawing 2 (Bore Bank) says, as read

- **Main deck, port side casing aft**, from aft: "Fixed ramp", "MGO day" and "MGO settl." tanks, "Aux. eng." (two sets
  drawn), "Eng. workshop", "Deck stores" and a WC; inboard of these "Cargo contr. room", "Casing", two rooms marked "Air"
  and emergency exits ("Em. ex."). The stern ramp is drawn lowered, with a second narrow ramp to the weather deck.
- **Weather deck, accommodation forward**: "Fire station", officers' day room, crew day room, a wardrobe and changing
  room at the entrance from the deck, galley, pantry and two mess rooms; windlasses and mooring winches on the forecastle.
- Figures printed on page 1 [B]: stern ramp to main deck 16.00 x 13.60 m, 100 t; stern ramp to weather deck 16.00 x
  4.00 m; fixed ramps main deck to lower hold and main deck to weather deck, slope under 8 degrees; free height main
  deck 5.00 m, lower hold 4.60 m; lane metres 577 weather deck, 616 main deck, 318 lower hold, 1,511 in all; 12 drivers
  in 6 cabins.

## 2. The ship "Mont Ventoux"

**Identity: IMO 9129586, built 1996 as Lindarosa by Cantiere Navale Visentini, Donada (Porto Viro), Italy, yard number
170** [F][FS]. She has been called Mont Ventoux since October 2005. She was transferred to the Danish flag with home port
København on 14 March 2019 and sold to CMA CGM, Bahamas flag, home port Nassau, on 17 November 2021 [F]. Lifebuoys
lettered "MONT VENTOUX, KØBENHAVN" therefore date the owner's photographs to between March 2019 and November 2021 (my
inference from those two dates).

A second ship carried the same name earlier and must not be confused with her: IMO 9138783, built 1998 by Società
Esercizio Cantieri, Viareggio, as Stena Ausonia, then Sea Centurion, named Mont Ventoux from January 2003 to August 2005
on charter to Sudcargo, with four Sulzer engines (8ZA40S per [F], 8ZAL40S per [K]). Her [F] history names London, Nantes
and Limassol as home ports, never København.

### 3c. Three ships to base a 125 m classic on

1. **Mashala class (Rickmers, 1977)**: 116.30 x 17.40 x 5.38 m, two MaK 8M453AK with gearboxes and controllable pitch
   propellers. The only one with a readable GA (drawing 1) and a full machinery description [MM].
2. **Bison class (Sietas, 1975)**: 125.00 x 19.05 m, two Deutz 12M540 of 8,825 kW in all, 12 drivers. The right length.
3. **European Gateway class (Schichau Unterweser, 1975)**: 117.8 x 20.3 x 5.8 m, depth 11.65 m, two Stork-Werkspoor
   9TM410, twin side funnels, a crew of 36 and berths for drivers. The model for the drivers' accommodation.

Coutances (1978, 109.7 m, two MaK 9M453, 58 drivers, internal lift) is the fourth choice.

## 4. The general arrangement, keel up

Each line names the ship the fact comes from. Where no source was found the line says n/v.

**Double bottom and tanks.**
- Mashala class: water ballast tanks in the double bottom and wings, heavy fuel tanks under the lower hold, an aft peak
  tank around the steering gear, a fore peak tank [GA-R]. Riverdance normally carried only 158 t of ballast in No. 3 port
  and starboard; her sister carried 539 t, mostly in the fore peak [MR].
- Heeling: two heeling tanks, No. 13 port and starboard, joined by a 300 mm cross-over pipe with a reversible pump of
  600 m3/h, run in automatic mode in every port; non-return valves in the tanks and manual block valves in the engine
  room workshop, shut at sea [MR].
- Ballast valves on Riverdance were hydraulic, worked from a hand pump and a control panel without mimic, as built in
  1977. Sounding pipes for ballast and bunkers stood on the cargo decks [MR].
- Searunner: wing tanks for list, two of them worked from the cargo office aft on the port side of the main deck, all
  other ballast from the engine control room. She sailed with 441 m3 of heavy fuel and about 100 m3 of diesel [SHK].
- Larger ship: a double hull throughout the hold below the main trailer deck, with ventilation ducts built into it; two
  ballast pumps of 500 m3/h; a ballast control room on the main trailer deck near the stern port, with a graphic panel of
  all ballast and fuel tank levels [SA].

**Lower hold.**
- Mashala class: a lower hold forward of the engine room, built for cars and no longer used; reached from the main deck
  forward; port and starboard access hatches in the main deck with small coamings; its own vents with weathertight flaps
  on the weather deck; a bilge alarm shared with the bow thruster room [MR][MRA][GA-R].
- Searunner: the tank top is a full trailer deck reached by a cargo lift of 50 t safe working load from the main deck
  [SHK]. Coutances and Dana Maxima also used lifts [D][MRep]. Seaspeed Arabia had four: 19.0 x 6.6 m of 85 t and
  18.8 x 3.2 m of 50 t to the upper deck, 19.0 x 3.8 m of 60 t and 18.8 x 3.2 m of 50 t to the lower decks [SA].
- Later ships use a fixed ramp instead: slope under 8 degrees (Bore Bank) or 6 degrees (Bore Sea) [B]; 7.1 to 7.3
  degrees on the teaching plan, with a watertight sliding door on the tank top [LNU]; 3.7 m wide with an end-hinged
  watertight cover on Stena Forwarder [N].
- A warning from the period: Tor Gothia's class had an open ro-ro deck below the waterline over 60% of the length, which
  subdivision rules for cargo ships over 100 m have forbidden for new ships since 1 February 1992 [MG].

**Engine room.**
- Aft, under the main trailer deck. On the Mashala class the depth to that deck is 5.70 m, so the engines stand in a
  space less than 5.7 m high above the keel (my inference from the drawing), with a platform deck for the control room
  and workshop [GA-R]. This is why
  the type uses medium-speed engines: "more suitable than slow speed diesels for roll-on/roll-off ships with loading
  ports at the stern" [SA].
- The casing does not rise on the centre. "Engine casings and funnels are divided into two very thin constructions along
  the side shell" [SA]. The teaching plan shows an "E.R. casing" at each side of the upper deck aft with a "ventilation
  house" ahead of each [LNU]. Photographs show twin side funnels on the European class, the Tor Gothia class and Stena
  Carrier, and one offset funnel on Mashala, Bison, Dana Maxima and Ibex [P]. On Boogabilla (1978) the exhaust went to
  the starboard side and up an offset funnel that was also the stern ramp's post [MRep].
- Rooms that do not fit below go into the side casing at main deck level: auxiliary engines, MGO day and settling tanks,
  engine workshop, deck stores and the cargo control room on Bore Bank [B]; engine control room, workshop and storeroom
  under the fixed ramp on Stena Forwarder [N].
- Engine control room: on the platform deck at the forward end of the engine room on the Mashala class, next to the
  workshop [GA-R][MR]. It was the master station for the propeller pitch: control could be passed to the bridge only
  from there [MM].

**Main trailer deck.**
- Free height: 5.8 m (Searunner) [SHK]; 5.00 m (Bore Bank, Seagard) [B]; 6.8 m (teaching plan) [LNU]; 7.20 m (Estraden)
  [B]. On the Mashala class the main deck is 6.50 m below the upper deck, moulded [GA-R].
- Lanes are 2.9 m wide on Seaspeed Arabia [SA], 2.97 m on Bore Sea [B], 3 m on the teaching plan [LNU].
- Deck strength on the teaching plan: 7 t/m2, axle load 10 t, bogie load 14 t [LNU]. Bore Bank: 5.0 t/m2 [B].
- Along the sides stand the casings, stair trunks and escapes: the teaching plan marks each "ESC" trunk from the tank
  top and lower hold to the upper deck, and a staircase at each corner [LNU].
- Scuppers with non-return valves drain the deck overboard; on the Searunner a bunker port also opened at this level
  [SHK].

**Upper deck and weather deck.**
- Reached by a fixed internal ramp (Mashala class [GA-R][MRA], Bore Bank [B]) or, where a second upper level exists,
  by a hoistable ramp that is raised and lashed for sea (Searunner, 50 t [SHK]; Estraden, 62.2 x 4.50 m, 140 t [B]).
  Bore Bank also has a separate stern ramp of 16.00 x 4.00 m straight to the weather deck [B].
- Under the house the height is less: "heights under the accommodation block being restricted" (Riverdance) [MR]; 4.25
  m on Finnbirch's upper deck [SHK]; 4.60 m under the superstructure on Estraden and Seagard [B]; 5.0 m on the teaching
  plan [LNU].
- An enclosed upper deck has large openings in the ship's side, closed on Finnbirch with plywood boards [SHK].

**Accommodation.** On the Mashala class, four decks forward [GA-R]:
- 1st superstructure deck: ratings' cabins (cook, seamen, bosun), galley, pantry, crew mess, crew recreation room,
  officers' mess, provision rooms, and in a separate after block two passenger cabins with the passengers' lounge and
  dining area.
- 2nd superstructure deck: master, deck officers and engineers in single cabins along the sides, ship's office and air
  conditioning room on the centre, four more passenger cabins aft, a motor lifeboat on each side.
- 3rd superstructure deck: wheelhouse. 4th: its top.
- Bore Bank adds a fire station, separate day rooms for officers and crew, and a changing room at the deck entrance [B].
- Seaspeed Arabia berthed 10 officers, 25 crew and 12 passengers, with a lift from the main trailer deck to the boat
  deck and a sewage plant and garbage unit under the deckhouse [SA].
- Drivers: 12 in 6 cabins on all the Bore ships [B]; two-berth cabins, a sauna and a day lounge for drivers on Tor Gothia
  (Commercial Motor, 15 October 1976) (summary).
- Hospital, laundry, offices for the cargo: no label could be read with certainty on the drawings. n/v.

**Bridge.** Wheelhouse with a control position at each wing (Mashala class) [GA-R]; pitch levers and bow thruster
controls at the centre and at both wings [MM]. Finnbirch's bridge had enclosed wings and was wide but not deep, with a
door to an open bridge deck behind each wing [SHK]. Stena Gothica's was forward with open wings and helm and engine
controls on each [MG].

**Mooring decks.** Aft: on the Searunner a poop mooring station on each side at a level between the main and upper
decks [SHK]; on the Mashala class winches on the upper deck at the stern [GA-R]. Forward: the forecastle with windlasses
ahead of the house [B]; chain lockers and stores below it [GA-R].

**Steering gear.** A compartment right aft at tank top level between the after ballast tanks (Mashala class) [GA-R],
with two steering motors, a hydraulic header tank and twin spade rudders [MM][MR]. On the Searunner it lay below the
hydraulic pump room and was entered down a trunk [SHK].

**Bow thruster room.** Forward of the lower hold, entered by a hatch, with a bilge alarm (Mashala class) [MR][MRA].

**Emergency generator.** In its own compartment in the superstructure with a telephone at its door; on Moondance it also
drove the emergency fire pump through a clutch [MM]. On the Riverdance drawing the room is on the 1st superstructure
deck, port side aft (reading uncertain) [GA-R].

**CO2 room and fire fighting.** Cargo ro-ros of the 1970s protected their closed cargo spaces with CO2 and smoke
detection: Seaspeed Arabia [SA], Boogabilla (low-pressure CO2 with gas detection) [MRep]. A "fire station" appears on
Bore Bank and on the teaching plan [B][LNU]. A drencher room was not found on any of these cargo ships: n/v. The
position of the CO2 room on a ship of 110 to 140 m: n/v.

**Hydraulic rooms for the ramps.** Searunner: a hydraulic pump room on the main deck aft on the port side, under the
poop mooring station, with a watertight door to the cargo deck [SHK]. Seaspeed Arabia: the stern ramp is worked by an
electro-hydraulic winch on the upper deck from a control stand with a window on the main trailer deck; each lift has a
power unit with two pumps, so that one pump gives half speed [SA].

**Ventilation of the cargo decks.**
- Air changes: 30 an hour in port and 10 at sea (Estraden) [B]; 20 to 30 while working cargo and 10 at sea, by axial
  fans, 36 of them (Seaspeed Arabia) [SA]; 30, 25 and 20 an hour on the three lower decks (Boogabilla) [MRep].
- Where the air comes in: closable intake trunks along the sides of the upper deck amidships for the main deck, and
  large fan intakes just aft of the forecastle for the main deck and tank top (Searunner) [SHK]; open trunk vents with
  grilles under the forward accommodation and mushroom vents on the after weather deck (Riverdance) [MRA]; fans on the
  weather deck forward and at both sides with ducts in the double hull (Boogabilla) [MRep].

**Bunker station, pilot doors, provision crane.** A bunker port in the side at main deck level on the Searunner [SHK]. A
side door each side of the main deck on the Riverdance drawing, labelled as pilot door and bunker station (reading
uncertain) [GA-R]. Provision cranes: n/v, no source found.

**Lashing.**
- Securing points in the deck: not more than 2.5 m apart fore and aft, 2.8 to 3.0 m apart athwartships, each good for
  120 kN; lashings of chain of not less than 120 kN; an uncoupled semi-trailer stands on a trestle, not on its landing
  legs; wheels chocked [IMO]. This guideline dates from 1985, later than every ship in section 3.
- In service: six web lashings of 6 t on a semi-trailer on the tank top and main deck, six to eight chains of 10 t on the
  upper and weather decks (Finnbirch) [SHK]; trailers on trestles, lashings checked every 30 minutes on her last passage
  (Riverdance) [MR].
- Fittings: lashing points let into the deck with cups below them, which rusted through over the tanks on Finnbirch
  [SHK]; lashing pots on all vehicle decks (Tor Magnolia) [WE]; loose gear, chains and stacking pieces kept in boxes on
  the side shell and in wagons (Seaspeed Arabia) [SA].

**Gears and propellers.** Reduction gearboxes and Escher Wyss 4-bladed controllable pitch propellers on the Mashala
class, pitch range 32 degrees ahead and 23 astern, two screw pumps for the pitch hydraulics, a pneumatic signal from the
levers to the pitch setter, emergency hand control at the oil transfer box on each shaft [MM]. Seaspeed Arabia: a
reduction gear with multi-disc oil clutches and a Kawasaki-Escher Wyss propeller of 6.4 m turning at 122 rpm, the engines
run at constant speed because they drive the generators [SA]. The Searunners had fixed pitch propellers [K].

**Electrical plant.**
- Mashala class: two diesel alternators (Deutz BA 8M 816 with AEG type DKBH, 270 kW, 430 A, 440 V, 60 Hz) and two
  shaft generators of 550 kW that give 60 Hz at 600 engine rpm. At sea one shaft generator carried the ship; when
  manoeuvring the two diesel sets ran in parallel on the main bus and one shaft generator fed the bow thruster alone.
  Shaft generators could not run in parallel. Emergency set: Lister 3-cylinder, air cooled, AEG 20 kW, automatic start,
  feeding an air compressor, a steering motor, a fuel pump and emergency lighting; 24 V batteries for radio, fire alarm
  and control circuits [MM].
- Seaspeed Arabia: two main-engine-driven generators of 2,400 kVA and two auxiliary diesel generators; the load includes
  three thrusters, two fin stabilisers, four lifts, 36 hold fans and 75 reefer sockets [SA].
- Later ships for comparison: two 600 kW sets and a 1,432 kW shaft generator (Bore Bank); two Caterpillar 3508B and a
  1,200 kW shaft generator (Estraden) [B]; two MAN B&W 7L28/32 of 3,000 kW in all (Sea Centurion) [K].

**Thrusters.** One Jastram BU80F bow thruster of 441 kW with a fixed pitch propeller and a reversing motor, three power
steps, worked from the bridge and both wings (Mashala class) [MM]. Three Kawasaki thrusters of 470 kW with controllable
pitch, two forward and one aft (Seaspeed Arabia) [SA]. Bow 720 kW and stern 495 kW (Bore Bank) [B].

**Stabilising and heeling.** Heeling tanks with a 600 m3/h reversible pump (Riverdance) [MR]. Fin stabilisers on Dana
Maxima [MRep] and retractable Brown Brothers fins on Seaspeed Arabia [SA]. None on the Searunners, whose bilge keels
were also lost in ice [SHK]. Later: Intering anti-heeling and stabiliser tanks (Bore Bank), Framo anti-heeling (Bore
Sea) [B], flume tanks added to Dana Futura (1996) in 2000 [F].

**Cooling, fuel and other systems.** Mashala class: main engines burn intermediate fuel oil or diesel; fresh water and
lubricating oil are cooled by sea water through coolers with strainers; electrically driven cooling, lubricating oil
and fuel pumps; a separate harbour sea water pump for the generators; a boiler with a header tank; an engine room
workshop; no machinery data logger, readings kept by hand in the log [MM]. Seaspeed Arabia could run 14 hours with the
engine room unattended (Lloyd's UMS) [SA].

**Not found for a ro-ro freight ship of this era**: makes and sizes of the oil-fired and exhaust gas boilers, purifiers,
starting air compressors, fresh water generator, sewage plant and steering gear. n/v. The companion file [E] gives such
a list for a ro-pax of 1980 from the Estonia report: use it as the nearest evidence, and mark what is taken from it.
