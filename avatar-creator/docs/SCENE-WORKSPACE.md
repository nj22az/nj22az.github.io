# Johansson Studio: 3D scenes and comics

The standalone `/avatar-creator/` has three modes: Edit character, Build scene and Make comic. Build scene guides the author through location, characters, poses and placement, dialogue, and export.

The preview now uses the actual town and room geometry, rather than location photographs with avatar sprites painted over them. Avatar meshes, furniture and architecture share one Three.js scene, perspective camera, lighting and depth buffer. Location photographs are only thumbnails. PNGs and comic panels capture this same 3D render; speech bubbles and captions are added on a separate transparent layer, anchored to projected character positions. Hidden characters do not display floating dialogue.

**Controlling characters (2026-10-11).** A cast strip under the picture has one chip per character (a portrait and the name): tap to select (the selected chip has a green edge and tint, nothing else), ✕ to remove, with an Undo toast for seven seconds (also Ctrl/Cmd+Z). In the picture, a tap selects (a fingertip within 16 px of a character counts), a drag always moves, a two-finger twist turns, and a tap on empty space deselects. A small toolbar beside the selected character (placed by `scene-controls.mjs`: below the feet, else above the head, else beside or over the legs, always inside the picture and never over the face) turns left or right to the next 45° stop around facing the camera, faces the camera, sits down on the nearest free seat within 2.5 m (else on the ground) or stands up, and removes. Keys: Q/E or [ ] turn to the next 15° stop, the arrow keys nudge relative to the picture (Shift: further), Delete removes, Escape deselects. Pose and expression are labelled buttons in groups, read from World's own lists (`scene-catalogue.mjs`: animate.js and face.js), so new World poses and faces appear without a Studio change; the expression buttons show the selected character's own face.

**Snap into place (2026-10-11).** A drag lifts a character and puts them down where they snap into place; they never walk there, so staff can be put behind a counter. The feet stay where they were on the picture relative to the finger, and wherever they land is free (no wall, furniture or other character), on level floor, within 12 m of the location's centre (what a saved scene keeps) and seen by the camera (the head, or the seat, not hidden by scenery). Near a free seat they sit while still under the finger, facing the way the seat faces, and a seated pose such as Drink goes with them from stool to stool; dragged off, they stand as they stood before, facing the way they sat. Near a counter (a top 0.73–1.15 m above the floor in front of it, from either side) they stand 0.23·size + 0.03 m off its face, facing it, in 5 cm steps along it. In front of a standing character they face them (0.95 × the two sizes' mean away); beside them (0.70 ×) they face the same way. A seat beats a person and a person a counter, by a tenth of the catch distance; the catch is 44 px for a finger and 24 px for a mouse, clamped per kind to between 0.12 and 0.45 m on the ground, and holds until 1.5 times as far. With nothing near, they go exactly where dropped (to the centimetre), or to the nearest free place within 0.6 m (sliding along walls and counters), or stay where they last landed. The only sign is the existing outline: dashed while placing freely, solid while something has caught them, and it moves at once. Letting go keeps exactly what is shown; sitting down or standing up shows the Undo toast with one sentence ("Thuận sits at the ramen counter 3."), a counter or a person a line in the status. Turning steps around facing the camera: Q/E and [ ] 15°, the toolbar's turn buttons to the next 45° (front, three-quarter, profile, three-quarter back, back), a two-finger twist 15° (changing 3° past halfway) and catching on facing the nearest character within 2.5 m or, at a counter, facing it. The Turn slider (5°) stays exact. "Snap into place" in Pose & position turns the magnets and twist stops off (kept per viewer in `nj-studio-snap`); on a keyboard, Alt/Option while dragging or twisting does the same for that gesture. Arrow keys and the Left/right and Near/far sliders never use magnets; they walk, sliding along whatever stops them. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes the last placement change or removal (up to 20, this location only). Seats are saved by a stable key (World's seat id, else the seat's place, `@x,z`); a scene saved with the older `seat-N` finds its seat and is saved again with the key. Repeated seat labels are numbered left to right ("Sit at the ramen counter 1…5"); seats someone else uses are shown taken. The rules are pure and tested in `scene-snap.mjs` (for the room, building and island builders later: `gridPoint` and a `grid` option are already there); targets come from `scene-world.js` (`snapTargets`), which reads World's seats, colliders, floors and meshes and changes nothing in World. During a drag, visibility comes from one depth picture of the scenery read back once (a ray costs up to 40 ms in the dining rooms' merged meshes).

**Saving (2026-10-11).** iOS Safari ignores a download or share started after an `await`, and a download lands in Files, not Photos. So the picture, the comic page and each panel are made ahead of time (`studio-share.mjs`) whenever they change; the Save control is a real `<a download>` to the ready file, and on a touch device that can share files the tap opens the share sheet instead (Save Image puts it in Photos). A tap that comes before the file is ready makes it on the spot, inside the tap. Preview shows the picture full screen to press and hold (`-webkit-touch-callout:default`, since World's base CSS turns the callout off). The project file is made in the tap and shared (Save to Files) or downloaded.

Dragging raycasts a character mesh and puts it down where it snaps into place (above). Left/right and near/far sliders and arrow keys walk it instead: movement follows the town's walking routes and drawn floor slabs, slides along furniture, walls, other characters and ground boundaries, and samples the full movement path to prevent crossing thin walls. Characters remain grounded. The furniture selector uses authored seat positions, facing directions and seat heights; occupied seats cannot be selected twice. Standing or moving leaves the seat. Camera turn, height and distance controls compose a shared perspective view; camera rays shorten the camera path at solid scenery.

The twelve locations reuse the original world builders and dining-room asset. The exterior town is retained across outdoor location changes. Original game simulation is not started. The standalone page's base URL points asset requests to the existing game assets, while Studio scripts and styles use absolute paths. The game source and saved-game format are unchanged.

Scene schema version 2 stores X/Z placement in metres, physical scale, seat identity and camera controls. Version 1 imports preserve recipes, poses and text, resetting old flat-image placement to safe ground positions. The existing scene storage key remains compatible. Captured comic panels stay as PNGs, including older imported panels; recomposing a panel produces a new 3D capture. Downloadable projects preserve scene state and up to four panels.

## Verification

Node checks cover the pose catalogue, toolbar placement, twist and nudge maths, sharing and ready files, removal and Undo, seated heights, save/import migration, limits, collision sampling, floor/overhead exclusions, all forty Johansson/Thuận pose combinations, and the actual twelve location builders. Location checks load shipped local assets, verify perspective cameras, ground placement, six non-overlapping characters, and authored seating. Syntax and local import/asset paths are checked. Browser acceptance (`tests/studio.browser.mjs`, Playwright with Chromium) covers adding, selecting, turning, twisting, dragging, sitting, removing and undoing, the PNG, comic and project saves on the download path and (an emulated iPad with a share sheet) the share path inside the tap, and the preview. Mobile Safari's share sheet, press-and-hold saving and WebGL performance still need the iPad.
