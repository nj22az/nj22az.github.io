Johansson PF-82

A synthesiser built around the Polivoks filter, simulated part by part, imperfections included.

The Polivoks gets its growl from an unusual filter: two programmable op-amps run without feedback as integrators, so their slew rate is the filter. The PF-82 simulates clarionut’s Eurorack version of that circuit component by component and plays it with two oscillators, two envelopes and an LFO.

  1. Power on, top right of the panel.
  2. Play the keys with the mouse, a touch screen, the A–L row (Z/X for octave) or a MIDI keyboard, or pick a song.
  3. Turn FREQUENCY and RESONANCE while it plays. Point at any control to see what it does.
What do the words on the panel mean?
Sound
A saved setting of every knob. Synth players call it a patch, after the cables of early synthesisers.
VCO, oscillator
Makes the raw tone, buzzy (saw), hollow (square), soft (triangle) or nasal (pulse). The PF-82 has two.
Mixer
Sets how much of each oscillator, and of noise, goes on to the filter.
VCF, filter
Takes brightness away from the tone. This is the Polivoks part. FREQUENCY sets how bright, RESONANCE makes it ring and, turned right up, whistle by itself.
VCA, amplifier
Sets how loud the sound is from moment to moment.
Envelope (ENV, ADSR)
How something moves each time you press a key: Attack (how fast it rises), Decay (how fast it falls), Sustain (the level it holds), Release (how long it fades after you let go). ENV 1 moves the filter, ENV 2 the loudness.
LFO
A slow wobble: vibrato on the pitch, or a wah on the filter.
CV
Control voltage: an electrical signal that turns a knob for you, as ENV 1 turns FREQUENCY on every note.
Jacks
The sockets on the filter. The lit OUT jack is the one you hear.
Glide, legato
Notes slide into each other; with legato on, a new note does not restart the envelopes.
Imperfections
What makes every real unit a little different: parts that are not exact, warming up, hum and hiss, worn knobs.

How the filter works

Sakura Shōten’s jingles

Five little tunes for Sakura Shōten, the konbini in Minato: the door melody, the till, the shop jingle between songs on its radio, the morning wake-up as the shutter goes up and the waltz that closes the day. Each has its moment in the shop’s hours and a reason for its notes. All are new tunes, and all keep to the Ryukyu scale of Okinawan island songs, C E F G B.

The recordings were made on the shop’s own PF-82, serial 13715, picked for its two oscillators, which stay within 2 cents of each other from C3 to C6. “Play on the PF-82” plays the same notes on yours, with your unit’s own tuning; set its serial under Imperfections to hear the shop’s. The closing waltz loops.

These are the first versions. The shop now plays brighter, easier ones, written after the manner of 1980s game music, on Johansson Denki’s four-voice SC-90 sound chip; these stay here for anyone who wants the PF-82’s sound.

    What is simulated

    • Exponential converter. Q1 (2N3904) sits in U1B’s feedback with R8 across its base and emitter, so the summed input current sets VBE and the cutoff doubles every 5.9 µA. That is 0.594 V on CV2: a 1 V/octave keyboard over-tracks 1.68 times. Nothing compensates for temperature, so the cutoff rises about 1.3 octaves per 10 °C.
    • The core. Two MC1776 run open loop as integrators behind 1/50 and 1/48 dividers. The tanh of their input pairs is the slew limit: loud signals and high resonance bend both the pitch and the shape. Their outputs stop at the ±12 V rails.
    • Resonance. RV4 feeds the band-pass back as damping. The op-amps’ second pole adds a little negative damping, so the filter breaks into oscillation near the end of the knob and, turned fully, screams into the rails almost an octave below its small-signal cutoff.
    • The two chips are not twins. Q1’s collector current divides between their Iset pins through R12 and R15, by the chips’ mismatch at low currents and by the resistors at high currents.
    • Outputs. AC coupling, the ×1.61 band-pass and ×1.33 low-pass buffers (they clip on ±12 V), and the equal-power LP/BP mixer around RV5.
    • A built unit. Resistors at 1 % or 5 %, pots at ±20 % with end resistance and pot loading, transistor and op-amp spreads and offsets, the regulator’s accuracy, warm-up, mains ripple on +12 V, hiss that rises as the cutoff falls, and worn wipers that lift for a moment when turned.
    • Not from the Polivoks. The oscillators, envelopes, LFO and amplifier around the filter are the PF-82’s own simple design, with their own small drift. Parasitic capacitance, ageing and the 776’s output stage at very low bias are not modelled.

    The song book
