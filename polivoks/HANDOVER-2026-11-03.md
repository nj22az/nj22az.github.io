## What was built

- **The model** (`polivoks/dsp/polivoks-dsp.js`): clarionut's Eurorack Polivoks filter (commit `9c155cf`) stage by
  stage: U1A attenuverter, U1B/Q1 uncompensated exponential converter, the Iset split through R12/R15, two MC1776
  open-loop integrators (tanh input pairs, rails, finite gain, second-pole negative damping), coupling, clipping
  buffers, the RV5 mixer; 4× (or 2×) oversampled trapezoidal solve, Kaiser half-band decimators. Units come from a
  serial number in three grades, with tolerances, drift, warm-up, mains ripple, hiss and worn pots.
- **The synthesiser** around it (our own design): two VCOs, noise, mixer, two ADSRs, LFO, VCA, glide, mono/duo, line in.
- **Songs** (`polivoks/songs.js`): seven public-domain tunes in our own single-line transcriptions (Grieg, Bach,
  Petzold, Beethoven ×2, Korobeiniki, Greensleeves), each with the patch it was arranged for, played by a
  sample-accurate sequencer inside the AudioWorklet. Levels balanced from offline renders.
- **Python illustrations** (`polivoks/art/draw.py`, standard library only): the signal-flow map on the panel
  (clickable, numbered like the modules) and the filter anatomy with its imperfections marked.
- **For synthesiser players**: modules numbered and named like the map (VCO, VCF, VCA, ENV, LFO), a help line for every
  control, the song bar, the song book.
- **Brand**: the **Johansson PF-82**, a Johansson Denki (ヨハンソン電機) instrument, World's maker of everything
  electric. "Polivoks" names only the simulated circuit; Formanta's instrument and clarionut's module are credited.
- **Website: unlisted.** For a short while `b206acd` put a seventh homepage card on `main` (entry `pf82`, a card
  image drawn by `draw.py`, a full-width odd-card layout, a guard expecting 7 cards, a sitemap entry). The owner then
  asked for no link, so all of it was reverted: `index.html`, `config.js`, `assets/js/project-explorer.js`,
  `css/project-explorer.css`, `sitemap.xml`, the homepage guard and its handover match `809a17b` again, and the card
  drawing is gone. The reverted card is in Git history (`b206acd`) if the owner wants it later.
- **MCP wiring** (`polivoks/agent.js`, `polivoks/mcp/server.mjs`, `polivoks/mcp/README.md`): a dependency-free stdio MCP
  server with ten tools: guide, list, check a song bar by bar, render a WAV with the exact engine, a share link
  (`#song=` on the live page), and live control (`pf82_open` serves the PF-82 on 127.0.0.1 with a token; the page
  connects by EventSource; `pf82_state/set/play/stop`). A song asked for while Power is off waits for the person's
  click. Songs from agents and links are kept under **Your songs** in that browser. `window.PF82` offers the same
  commands to browser agents. No WebMCP: this Chromium (153) has no `navigator.modelContext`, so nothing was built
  that could not be tested. The repository's other MCP servers declare the official SDK in
  `johansson-town/package.json` but it is not installed in fresh sessions, which is why they fail to start there.
- **Checks**: `.github/workflows/polivoks.yml` runs `npm test` and `art/draw.py --check`.
- **Found in passing, not changed:** the homepage guard (`office-home.yml`) overlays only six homepage files onto its

## Sakura Shōten jingles

- **What**: five new tunes in `polivoks/jingles/sakura.js`, each with its `when`, `where` and `why`, all in the Ryukyu
  scale (C E F G B) for Minato's Okinawan setting, none imitating a shop chain's melody: the door melody (1.7 s), the
  till "pi-pon" (0.5 s), the shop jingle for the radio (7.4 s; the hook sings "sa-ku-ra-shō-ten" with a kachāshī
  swing), good morning at 09:00 (4.1 s; never on Saturday, the closing day), and the closing waltz from 20:00 to the
  shutter at 20:30 (30 s, loops). The hours follow `johansson-town/src/commerce/sakura-rota.js`; the door melody's
  place matches the small office's record ("hears the door chime through the open door").
- **Rendered** by `node polivoks/jingles/render.mjs` on the shop's unit (`SHOP_UNIT`, serial 13715, modern, 22 °C, no
  warm-up) into `polivoks/jingles/sakura/`: WAV masters (48 kHz, 16-bit, −1 dBFS peak), MP3 copies, `manifest.json`.
  Deterministic; the waltz loops without a click (seam 0.002 against a largest step of 0.27).
- **Tuning**: on serial 1982 the oscillators are −12 and +32 cents at C5, so the door melody was sour (+28 cents).
  Serial 13715 was chosen as the best-tracking of the first 30,000 (both oscillators within 2 cents, C3–C6). Measured
  on the renders: every note within 11 cents.
- **Engine bug found and fixed**: the oscillators started half a period apart (phases 0.13 and 0.61), so two unison
  saws cancelled the odd harmonics and a note sounded an octave up until they drifted (for ever on an ideal unit; on
  the page, "Duo brass" on an ideal unit played every single key an octave high). Both now start at 0.13; a DSP test
  fails on the old phases. Built-in song levels were balanced before the fix; on a real unit the change only moves
  where the two oscillators' slow beating starts.
- **On the page**: a "Sakura Shōten's jingles" section (players for the shop's recordings; "Play on the PF-82" plays
  the same notes on the listener's unit) and a song-menu group. **Agents**: `pf82_list` lists them; `pf82_render { id }`
  renders on the shop's unit unless given a serial.
- **Not done, by design**: wiring them into Johansson World or the Sakura game (the door, the till, the radio, 09:00 and
  20:00 are the obvious hooks). That waits for the owner's word.
- **Nobody has listened yet.** Pitch, harmonics, loop seam and lengths were measured; whether they are fun is the
  owner's call.

## Johansson World and the Sakura game (the owner's question; not built)

World makes its sound with Web Audio already: synthesised concert voices and band (`johansson-town/src/music/audio.js`),
a synthesised shore and night (`src/audio/`), WAV samples for effects (`src/audio/town-audio.js`). The PF-82 engine
fits as an **instrument and a jingle maker**, not as the whole sound engine:

- It is one engine-independent ES module (no DOM), so World could create a PF-82 AudioWorklet for in-world
  electronics (Tetsuo's CFS-9, a shop keyboard, karaoke backing) behind a URL flag, as prototypes go
  (AGENTS.md: Codex prototypes on `development`).
- It runs in Node, so the Sakura game's door chime, till beeps, jingles and radio songs can be **rendered at build time
  to WAV/OGG** with this exact engine and loaded like today's samples: zero runtime cost on the Steam Deck.
- Budgets: one voice costs about a tenth of a desktop core at 4×; use 2× in games, render offline where possible.
- Not its job: footsteps, doors, voices, ambience, spatial mixing. Those stay with World's audio modules.
