## What was built

- **The chip** (`sc-90/chip.js`): two band-limited pulse voices (12.5/25/50 %), a 4-bit 32-step triangle, a 15-bit
  noise register shaped into kick, snare and hi-hats; pitches from a 4 MHz clock; S-curve envelopes, delayed vibrato,
  swing, echo; a speaker-like output stage; `renderPiece` (loop, trim, level) shared by Node and the browser.
- **Five new jingles** (`sc-90/sakura.js`) in C major, written so that at every beat each voice sounds a note of that
  bar's chord, all opening or built on the shop's motif "sa-ku-ra shō-ten" (E G A G C, Japanese major pentatonic):
  door chime, till "pi-pon", shop jingle (radio), good morning (09:00), closing waltz (20:00–20:30, loops). Hours from
  `sakura-rota.js`. All new melodies; Kondo is the manner only.
- **Renders** (`node sc-90/render.mjs`): WAV masters and MP3 copies in `sc-90/sakura/`, all −15 dBFS rms, peaks under
  −1 dBFS; deterministic.
- **The page** (`sc-90/index.html`, `page.js`, `sc-90.css`): the recordings, and the chip playing each jingle live in the
  browser with its voices switchable (melody, harmony/echo/chords, bass, drums). `window.SC90` = { jingles, play, stop }.
- **Tests and CI**: `cd sc-90 && npm test`, 13 checks; `.github/workflows/sc-90.yml`.
- The PF-82 page's jingle section now says these are the first versions and links to the SC-90 (one sentence).

## Checks actually run

- `npm test` in `sc-90/`: **13/13**. The harmony test was shown to fail on a planted wrong bass note (B under F).
- `npm test` in `polivoks/`: **48/48** (unchanged by this round).
- Spectrograms (numpy/matplotlib via uvx) of the door chime, shop jingle and waltz: bass, harmony and melody where
  written, clean harmonics; the only broadband marks are note onsets and hi-hats. ffmpeg's own spectrum picture
  mislabels its log axis and was not used as evidence.
- Chromium, local server: five cards, all five recordings served (200), live play of the shop jingle with the drums off
  (0.4 s to start) and of the looping waltz (0.6 s), stop works; no console errors; no overflow at 390 px. The PF-82
  page still has its five jingle cards and links to `/sc-90/`.

## Limits, honestly

- **Nobody has listened.** Pitch, harmony, levels, loop and spectra are measured; whether it is "pleasing for the ears"
  is the owner's call. The music was written by rule and by ear-in-theory, not auditioned.
- Mono only. No velocity or per-note volume; drums are three fixed sounds.
- Live playback renders the whole piece first (under a second), then plays; it is not a real-time synth.
- Safari and Firefox not checked.
