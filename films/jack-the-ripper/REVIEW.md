# Review of the fourth cut, and of the lip-sync

The owner: "Audit yourself you will decide when it's acceptable"; "how is the lipsync ? is it correct? does it look like
Polly actually is singing ?" On 13 November 2026 the fourth cut was gone through by sixteen independent reviewers (two
lenses for each 18 seconds: the figures; the picture and the edit), and every finding was looked at again on full-size
frames by a second reviewer told to refute it. **96 findings, 95 confirmed.** Several describe the same fault seen by two
reviewers. The reviewers saw stills only, not motion. Times are video seconds (song time + 6).

The full findings, with measurements, causes and untested suggested fixes, were in the cloud session's scratch folder
(`review4_confirmed.json`, `lipaudit.json`); this file is the durable record of what they found.

**Status.** The edit and set faults are being fixed for the fifth cut. The animation faults need shots drawn again and are
for the sixth cut, with the rebuilt lip-sync. Nothing below is fixed until this file says so.

**The answer to the owner's question: partly.** Mid-line, where the speech model's word times were right, her mouth
makes
the right shapes at the right moments and reads in stills as a woman singing these words. But:

- *word-timing.* Partly. No global shift is needed (the word starts lead the voice by a median of about 60 ms, which
  is the right direction for lip-sync) and about half of the 92 word starts I could check against the recording are
  within 80 ms. But a quarter are off by more than 200 ms, and the errors sit where they show most: 11 line-opening
  words start 0.3 to 1.3 s before the voice (the speech model stretched them back over the breath or silence), and
  lipsync.py then holds every word across any pause under 1.2 s, so between 6.4 s and 122 s the mouth comes fully to
  rest only 3 times while the singer pauses about 25 times. Mid-line it will read as singing; at line starts,
  between lines and on the last note Polly mouths in silence or stops before the voice does (seen in close-up at
  VIDEO 33.0 and 69.2).
- *curve-vs-voice.* The mouth is not late or early as a whole (whole-song lag +1 to +11 ms, about +28 to +45 ms once
  held at 12 pictures a second, and the film's sound sits 43 ms late, which cancels that), and it moves at the
  song's own syllable pace, so on the sung syllables it lands. But it does not follow the voice: it is open for 85%
  of the 116 s from the first word to the last, opens a median 0.35 s before the voice comes back after a pause,
  stays open through 81% of the pauses, and shuts in only 8 of 34 of them. The result reads as a mouth mouthing a
  word list rather than a singer breathing and phrasing; the worst cases are in close-ups ("Alone", before
  "Knives").
- *method.* The method is only half sound. It is real lip-sync (words spelled into sounds, lips shut on M/B/P, last
  vowel held, three working shapes plus teeth), but it is built to arrive with or after the sound, never before it;
  it keeps the mouth open through silences; it loses about a quarter of the lip closures to the 12-a-second
  sampling; and the rig can only make a smiling lens, so OO/OH and F/V do not read. As rendered it looks like a
  character mouthing along roughly in time, not like Polly singing. The problems below are in order of value: only
  the edit-timing lead (problem 3) can be applied without rendering frames again; every other fix needs Polly's
  frames re-rendered, and the rig changes (problem 6) need face_rig.py re-run first.

worst in
   the "Alone" close-up, where the mouth hangs open about 2.2 s with nobody singing, and before "Knives".
3. The last note: the mouth shuts at 122.0 s of the song while the voice holds to about 123.3 and a soft tail to

   a lip closure the voice does not make sits at 119.4 in the final close-up; she bows while still singing.
4. No lead: each picture shows the face at the start of its hold, so the mouth arrives with the sound, not before it.
5. Held notes are one frozen pose; loudness can enlarge the mouth a little but never close it.
6. At 12 pictures a second some M, B, P closures fall between pictures; tongue consonants snap the jaw (chatter).
7. The face can only make a smiling lens: no round O, no F or V, no tongue, teeth as one white strip; eyelids on a
blink
   are blank pale discs; head nods late with each syllable and there is no breath before a phrase.
