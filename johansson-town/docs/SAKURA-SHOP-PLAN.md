# Sakura Shōten: store audit and a plan to rebuild the floor

2 October 2026. Written as a konbini store manager would walk a shop on a visit (FamilyMart, Lawson and 7-Eleven
all use the same playbook). The shop stays Thuan's family *shōten*, with its wood, its crest and its cat. It
should sell like a 1997 konbini.

## The shop today

The sales floor is 13.6 × 7.8 m, about 106 m². That is the size of a real konbini, so the room is right and
the problem is what is in it. The door is at the centre of the front window and the counter runs along the
east wall.

## What a manager would write up

| # | Finding | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shelves are mostly bare.** There are 33 lines at 12 units each, on gondolas that could face 400 or more. Whole boards are empty, and a chiller column holds two rows of bottles. | An empty shelf says "closing down". In a konbini every facing is full, front-faced and pulled forward (前出し). |
| 2 | **Fresh food is in the drinks fridge.** Bento, sandwiches and pudding sit behind glass doors among the drinks. Plum rice balls are on a dry shelf at room temperature. | Konbini fresh food goes in an **open multi-deck chiller** (オープンケース), at reach height, without a door to open. Rice balls are the shop's best-seller and the reason people come in at lunch. |
| 3 | **Steamed buns are on the far west wall,** in a cabinet across the shop from the till. | Buns live in a **steamer on the counter**, next to the hot-snack case and the oden. They are an impulse buy at the till. |
| 4 | **The categories are shuffled.** Soap and detergent stand beside rice and curry. Notebooks and postcards are among the tinned food. Batteries sit with the toothpaste. | Customers navigate by category. Food, confectionery and daily goods each need their own run, with related lines next to each other. |
| 5 | **No "golden route".** The drinks are at the back (correct, it is the magnet), but nothing pulls you down the side walls or past the confectionery on the way back to the till. | The classic flow is: magazines along the window → round the wall to the drinks at the back → fresh food down the side → back up the gondolas → till by the door. |
| 6 | **Only one end cap is used.** Six gondola ends face the aisles; one holds the curry promotion. | End caps are the shop's best space: new items, the month's offer, seasonal goods, beer snacks next to the beer. |
| 7 | **There are no services.** No copy machine, no fax, no parcel desk, no public phone, no bins outside. | In 1997 these were half the reason to visit a konbini. On an island they would be more so: parcels to the main island, a fax for the fishermen's co-op. |
| 8 | **Visual noise.** Bunting across the whole ceiling, several hanging POP spinners, stand-ups by the door. | Too many signals and nothing stands out. One promotion band, POP only where the product is. (This also follows the site's "keep it clean" rule.) |
| 9 | **The mascot blocked the walk from the door to the till.** | Fixed: Jaga-bō now stands by the west window between the magazine rack and the plant. |

1. **Front window: life in the window.** The magazine and newspaper rack runs along the west window, so people
   reading are seen from the street. The copy machine and fax stand by the east window, near the till, where
   Thuan can help. The ice-cream chest moves to the back beside the drinks.
2. **The counter stays where it is** (rebuilt this week).
   - Two registers' worth of top: one till, one parcel and service end with a scale for parcels to the main island.
   - **Hot food together at the door end:** steamer for the buns, hot-snack case, oden.
   - Cigarettes behind; impulse lines (gum, batteries, phone cards, ferry tickets) along the counter's lip.
   - A painted queue line on the floor.
3. **Back wall: the drinks magnet.** The walk-in cold cabinet keeps its place and holds drinks only: tea, coffee,
   water, juice, cola, ramune, milk. It grows to five doors, with beer at the end nearest the counter.
4. **West wall: an open chiller for fresh food.** It replaces the bun cabinet. Rice balls at eye level,
   then bento, sandwiches, bread, yoghurt and pudding.
5. **Three gondolas in parallel, each one category,** with the aisles running from the window to the drinks:
   - *Pantry* (nearest the chiller): instant noodles, soup, curry, soy sauce, tins.
   - *Sweets* (middle, on the door's line): crisps, crackers, biscuits, chocolate, sweets. Children's lines go
     on the low shelves.
   - *Daily goods* (nearest the till): soap, detergent, tissues, toothpaste, batteries, stationery and postcards.
6. **Six end caps, all working.** Front ends carry the month's promotion, new items and the season (taken from
   the town calendar the window decorations already follow). Back ends carry beer snacks beside the beer, and
   a lunch-deal end beside the chiller.
7. **Full shelves.**
   - Each line is faced across its whole board, 3–4 deep, and pulled to the front.
   - Fifteen to twenty more lines fill the gaps: dagashi, rice crackers, cup sake, insect coils, film,
     cassette tapes, sun cream, sanitary goods, a 1997 Tamagotchi peg.
   - Restocking works the same way: a facing empties from the front, and the restock run fills it back.
8. **Clean signage.**
   - One seasonal band over the counter and one POP card per end cap.
   - Bunting over the counter only, or none.
   - Floor stickers only for the queue.
9. **Outside** (on the frontage): a green card phone, a red post box (already on the street), two bins
   (cans/bottles and burnables), and the bicycle rack.

## How it would be built

1. `sakura-layout.js`: put the three islands in parallel (one transform, as now) and regroup the bays by category.
2. A new `sakura-chiller.js`: an open multi-deck case on the west wall. It holds rice balls, bento,
   sandwiches, bread, yoghurt and pudding, which come out of the cold cabinet and the dry shelf.
3. The cold cabinet holds drinks only (the ice-cream chest stays by the window: there is no free wall beside the cabinet without blocking the restroom door).
4. Move the buns' stock to a counter steamer, and remove the bun cabinet.
5. A copy machine and fax by the east window. `shop-stock.js` gains the fill lines, with full-facing capacity per board.
6. Tests: every product sits on a board, every stand point is clear, the door-to-drinks-to-till route is walkable,
   no aisle is narrower than 0.9 m.

## Item browsing (being built now)

Pick any item off a shelf to look at it up close. Each line's packaging is drawn as a large, readable label:
brand, Japanese name, an illustration, the price and the small print. You can turn it, read the back,
then put it back or take it to the till. See `src/world/interiors/item-viewer.js`.

## Built (2 October 2026)

- Gondolas by category: pantry (west), sweets (middle, children's lines low), daily goods (east).
- An open chiller on the west wall for rice balls, bento, sandwiches, bread, pudding and yoghurt, in place
  of the bun cabinet. The cold cabinet holds drinks only, with beer in the column nearest the till.
- A bun steamer on the counter beside the hot case.
- Every line is faced 24 deep (the buns 12), each board holding its share in full rows. Facings are spaced
  by the pack's own size.
- Front end caps on the west and middle gondolas (a ramen week and the new crisps). The east gondola has
  none, because its end is the walk from the door to the till.
- A copy machine and fax by the east window.
- The bunting is gone, and one hanging card remains.
- Walkers who hit a corner fall back to the planner's own route (room-walk.js); the smoothed one had
  Thuan stuck at the west end cap with a carton.
